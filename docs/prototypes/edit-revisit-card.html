Four cards that do part of the work

Each card has one thing it does by itself. The Wallet card swipes sideways to choose its category. The flip card keeps the amount on the front and everything else on the back. The stamp card takes its category as a rubber stamp, with a postmark for when. The index card rolls a date stamper and inks in the next charges. Your sketch is there to compare against. ✓ is the only button, and it lights when the card is whole. Switching card keeps the bill you're halfway through. Edit marks put a pencil, a chevron or a pill on whatever is filled in and can still be changed, to see whether that makes it clear what you can tap. The three H cards hold your categories as a hand of cards where the keys were, with the guess raised. Play one, by tapping it or flicking it up, and it flies onto the bill: a Wallet card, a playing card with the category as its suit, or a Wallet stack you scrub through. The three F cards turn that round: the bill is ready, and you file it, dragging it (or tapping) into its category's pocket or into the Wallet stack. In F1 the pocket is the last step. In F2 filing only sets the category, and you pull the card back out to change it. In F3 the card slots in behind a category's strip. The last two settle what the ending is. In P1 there is no ✓: once the card has an amount and a name, a shelf of pockets rises where the keys were, and putting the card away is the save. The category is the last step, not a field on the card. In P2 you choose the category with the hand, ✓ saves, and the card then files itself into its pocket so you can see where it went. P3 does the same with the pockets as the picker: tapping a pocket dresses the card in its colour and shows a ghost of it in the pocket, and ✓ files it. In P1, P2 and P3, tapping a bill on the timeline opens it, rising out of its pocket. Editing changes the whole bill here; this charge or future charges isn't built.

What a browser can't show
  • The real iOS keyboard, its suggestions and its feel. The keyboard here is drawn.
  • The emoji keyboard. A grid stands in for it.
  • The repeat wheel. A list stands in for it, and the date stamper's rubber bands stand in for a wheel picker.
  • Haptics. The stamp's thunk is a vibration call, and iPhone Safari ignores it.
  • iOS's springs. The Wallet swipe and the flip are approximations of them.
  • Liquid Glass and Dynamic Type.
  • Guessing. Here it's a short list of words, not a model.
  • SF Symbols. The pencil and the up-down chevron are drawn stand-ins for them.
  • Real springs, and the iPhone's haptic ticks as you scrub the stack. The hand's springs are approximations.
  • Dragging a card and dropping it into a pocket on a real phone will feel different, especially the magnet and the lip.
  • Filing into pockets. That comes separately.
